Zwischenzug

15.Qg3 Nxf5 16.gxf5 Bxf5 17.Bxf5 17…Qxf5

A real game. White stopped calculating at 17.Bxf5, a piece up. The queen takes back.

Most lines don’t end where you stop calculating.

Zwischenzug trains one habit: write your line all the way to the last capture. Only then does the engine show where it really ends.

Start

What is waiting for you

  1. Your games. A game you played, cut into short lessons: the set-up moves that mattered, and the tactics hiding one move away.
  2. Training. Every day a few positions from real Lichess games near your rating. You set the rating once; it moves with your results.

How a position goes

  1. Look. Read the task above the board: who is to move, and what to find.
  2. Calculate without moving pieces. Take your time.
  3. Play or write your line, both sides, move by move, until nothing more can be taken. Tap a piece and a square, or type Nxd3+ like on Lichess.
  4. Lock it. You get the key, the idea in plain words, and then the engine's verdict: where your line stopped too early, or that it holds.

The first time you open the board, a short tour shows every button. You can replay it any time with Board tour at the bottom of the page.
